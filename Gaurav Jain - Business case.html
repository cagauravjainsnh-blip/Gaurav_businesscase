Modelling core

AI playbook: efficiency and productivity across the modelling process

AI is useful at every stage of a modelling engagement, but in different ways. The aim is to take time out of the mechanical work and put it into review and judgement. At EY-Parthenon we are working on developing agentic workflows for model build and model review. They are not yet in production.

How I would introduce it in the team

1Start where the risk is low

Review checks, documentation, data mapping and first-draft commentary. Quick wins that build trust in the tools.

2Build shared assets

A prompt and skills library based on our modelling standards, so juniors get consistent results and new joiners ramp up faster.

3Adopt agentic workflows as they mature

Bring in first-cut build and review agents once they work reliably on real engagements, not before.

From internal efficiency to tools we can offer clients

The first gains are internal. As the capability matures, the same skills open up a better presentation layer and, eventually, AI-powered tools we can offer to clients. Select a step.

White spaces I would look at, once the basics are in place

Investor return economics app

Distribution waterfalls, IRR and MOIC by investor class under different exit scenarios, with plain-language questions on the results.

Loan tape analytics tool

Upload a tape, get standard cuts, vintage curves and roll rates, and a first-draft summary of findings.

Model review assistant

Mechanical checks on a client model, with findings logged for a reviewer to confirm.

Scenario explorer

A client-facing front end on a model, so management can test their own assumptions.

Why this is now realistic: with AI, coding has become far more manageable. Through vibe coding, a modeller can prototype a working tool without a development team, as with this page. Anything a client relies on would still need proper testing, security and firm sign-off.

Loan tape analytics for buy-side credit deals

Credit portfolios run to hundreds of thousands of loans. A deep dive at loan and cohort level shows the buyer what portfolio averages hide, and links straight to price and deal terms. Select an area of analysis.

Price, structure and diligence questions

What it showed on a recent consumer lending deal

Most of the growth came from existing borrowers, with ticket size and tenor rising as the rate fell. Delinquency for those borrowers was low and improving as the book grew.

But once an account went past 30 days, very few came back. The buyer's questions moved to how growth was being priced, and how much of the book was one bucket away from a loss.

How I would start

On live deals where a tape is already in the data room, working with the diligence team, and building the toolkit as we go. It applies to banks, consumer and auto finance, BNPL, SME lending and credit funds.
